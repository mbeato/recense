<objective>
Close the input-cap half of WR-02 and produce the phase-closing evidence pass.

`62-VERIFICATION.md` confirmed by source read that nothing bounds `raw.bodyText` before `stripHiddenContent`
(`src/source/gmail-adapter.ts:362`) — the review's proposed `MAX_STRIP_INPUT_BYTES` was never adopted. Plan 62-14
removed the two quadratics that the report's own shape exercised, but it did NOT remove the last one: T-62-54,
`STYLE_BLOCK_RE`'s lazy `</style>` tail scan, measured during planning at **111 ms / 435 ms / 1,751 ms /
6,946 ms** for 512 KB / 1 MB / 2 MB / 4 MB. With no cap, the sender still chooses the size, so the sender still
chooses the cost.

**Fail-closed is not a preference here, it is the only safe option, and the plan requires it be disproven the
other way.** Hidden-content decisions are non-local: a `<style>` rule anywhere in the document can hide an element
anywhere else in it. So truncating the input and stripping the prefix leaves a class-hidden payload in the kept
prefix un-stripped whenever its hiding rule sits past the cut — a leak of exactly the kind roadmap SC #3 forbids,
introduced by a performance guard. The plan therefore requires a body over the cap to contribute ZERO
sender-controlled bytes, and requires a test that would catch the truncate-then-strip design if anyone
reintroduces it.

**Cap value comes from 62-14's measurements, not from a round number.** Plan 62-14 records Shape T (the only
remaining quadratic) at four sizes. A 1 MiB bound puts the worst known shape at ~435 ms. For context on the
legitimate-mail cost of the bound: Gmail itself clips message display around 102 KB, and `EpisodicStore.capContent`
keeps only 8 KB of the resulting episode downstream (D-58), so a >1 MiB HTML body is both vanishingly rare and
already mostly discarded. The executor re-measures and confirms before locking the value.

Purpose: the second half of a measured availability gap on attacker-controlled input, plus the evidence table the
next verifier will re-run. Output: a bounded, fail-closed body expression at the one call site; the design control
that disproves the rejected alternative; and a `dist/`-level reproduction of every finding this wave closed.
